Common Problems That Can Ruin a 3D Printable Model

Published: April 05, 2024 • 7 min read • 3D Printing

Even an aesthetically detailed 3D model can fail when processed by a slicer. Most failures stem from a few recurring geometric issues that are easy to overlook inside a 3D viewport.

1. Inverted Face Normals

Every polygonal face possesses a direction vector (normal) defining which side is front (external) and which is back (internal). When normals are inverted or mixed, the slicer interprets solid sections as empty voids. In Cura, PrusaSlicer, or Bambu Studio, this causes entire layer sections to vanish or generate erratic support pillars inside the model.

2. Zero-Clearance Mechanical Keys

When modeling interlocking keys or alignment pins for multi-part models, modelers often design keys with identical dimensions (for example, a 10.0 mm peg into a 10.0 mm slot). In the physical world, thermal expansion, layer stepping, and extrusion flow will prevent these parts from fitting together without heavy grinding.

Always integrate an intentional tolerance gap: between 0.2 mm and 0.35 mm on each mating side for standard FDM printing.

3. Trapped Resin Cavities Without Drainage

When hollowing models for resin printing to save material, modelers must ensure that internal hollow chambers include adequate escape vents near the base. A sealed internal void creates a suction-cup effect during printing, peeling parts off the build plate and permanently trapping uncured liquid resin inside.
